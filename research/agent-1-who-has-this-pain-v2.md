## Findings

### 1. Cara Howard — signed a flat lease, told a stricter notice clause was "exactly the same" as the draft she'd already read

**Document type:** Lease (residential tenancy, UK).
**Moment:** At signing — she asked a verification question right before signing,
which is the closest any of the four researchers have come to the pre-signature
moment, but she was given a false answer and signed anyway. The pain was then
discovered at exit.
**Tag: NEW.** This is the first verified first-person lease story either
research round has found.

**Verbatim quote:**
> "The issue is that the signed contract they have states that we have to give
> 2 month's notice. And yes, I did sign it. However, I signed it based on the
> fact that they had sent me via email, a specimen contract to read through,
> prior to going in and signing, and this clearly specifies 1 month notice."

And, in a follow-up reply six days later:
> "I appreciate that I signed the document that states 2 months, but I did
> question when signing, if it was the same as the contract they had sent me
> via email, and they told me it was exactly the same. I feel this change
> should have been pointed out. If I had known anything was different about
> it, I would have sat in their office for as long as it took and read every
> word, as I had done on the one they had emailed me."

**What happened:** Before going in to sign, her letting agent emailed her a
draft lease ("specimen contract") that said she only needed to give one
month's notice to end the tenancy. She read that draft. At the signing
appointment, the final paper lease had quietly changed to two months'
notice. She noticed something felt different enough to ask the agent
directly whether it matched the draft — and the agent told her it was
identical. It was not. She only found out for certain when she tried to
end the tenancy and the agent held her to the two-month term, meaning she
had to keep paying rent on a flat she'd already moved out of.

**Why this matters for Redline:** this is the single clearest piece of
evidence in either research round that someone will actively try to verify a
contract against an earlier version right before signing — "I did question
when signing, if it was the same." A tool that could have instantly shown her
the one sentence that changed would have answered her exact question, at the
exact moment she asked it.

**Source:** Harringay Online (a North London community forum), thread
"Advice needed on legal/contractual point with estate agent," posted by
"cara howard," May 1, 2013, with her follow-up reply on May 7, 2013.
https://harringayonline.com/xn/detail/844301:Comment:488593
(Quote confirmed by downloading the raw page and searching it directly —
not just the single AI-summarized fetch.)

### 2. "katiem188" on MoneySavingExpert — asking, before a family member signs, whether a new eviction clause in a renewal lease is legal

**Document type:** Lease (residential tenancy, UK).
**Moment: pre-signature.** The poster is asking for advice about a clause in a
contract that has not been signed yet, specifically because they are worried
about it.
**Tag: NEW — this is the pre-signature moment V1 could not find.**

**Verbatim quote** (confirmed by downloading the raw page):
> "the new contract they are wanting her to sign has a special added clause
> stating she can be issued a section 21 after a few months into the
> contract, i know the renters Bill changes in may 2026, but this means she
> could be issued a section 21 if she was to sign this contract before that
> bill becomes law."

**Plain-English context:** A Section 21 notice is a formal eviction notice in
England that lets a landlord end a tenancy without giving a reason. A new UK
law (the Renters' Rights Bill) was due to remove this kind of no-reason
eviction, so a landlord adding this clause to a renewal contract, timed before
the law changes, would lock the tenant into the old, weaker protection. The
poster is asking the forum — before their family member signs — whether this
is legal and what to do about it.

**Why this matters for Redline:** this directly answers V1's open question
"does the pre-signature moment exist for anyone?" It does, at least once,
clearly, in a lease. The person is not confused about jargon in general — they
spotted the specific clause and researched enough to connect it to a specific
upcoming law change. What they lack is confirmation of whether the clause is
enforceable and a way to act on that before the signature happens.

**Source:** MoneySavingExpert Forum, thread "tenancy advice," comment by
"katiem188," posted 15 November 2025.
https://forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6640315/tenancy-advice
(Quote confirmed by downloading the raw page and searching it directly.)

### 3. Mumsnet poster — a family member in her 70s pressured into signing a new tenancy on the spot, with a rent increase and no copy given

**Document type:** Lease (residential tenancy, UK).
**Moment:** During signing — described by a family member immediately
afterward, while the household is still trying to figure out whether it can be
undone. This sits right at the boundary between "pre-signature" and "just
signed," which is close enough to the moment V1 was missing that I'm including
it, flagged clearly as not a pure pre-signature account.
**Tag: NEW, with a caveat** — this is reported by a relative, not the person
who signed, so it is a secondhand account of the moment rather than a direct
first-person one.

**Verbatim quote** (confirmed by downloading the raw page):
> "He was polite but forceful and 'in a hurry', he needed her to sign the new
> agreement there and then. My MIL is in her 70s, lives on her own and was
> naturally flustered. She signed the doc, which included a rental increase
> from 500 to 600 p/m. He didn't leave her a copy. He also insisted on a new
> deposit (she hasn't received her deposit from her old landlord), which she
> paid."

**What happened:** After the rented property was sold, a representative of
the new landlord arrived with a new tenancy agreement and pressed the tenant
to sign immediately, without leaving time to read it or get advice. She
signed, agreeing to a rent increase and a new deposit, and was not given a
copy of what she'd signed. Her family is now trying to work out, after the
fact, whether any of it can be challenged.

**Why this matters for Redline:** this is a case where the barrier was not
understanding the contract — it was never getting the chance to read it
before being pressured to sign. That's a different problem than Redline
solves (Redline reads a document you already have; it can't help if you're
never given time to read it at all). I'm flagging this distinction because it
would be a mistake to read this story as full support for the product as
scoped.

**Source:** Mumsnet, "Legal matters" forum, thread "MIL has been strong-armed
into signing a tenancy contract," posted 30 March 2017.
https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/legal_matters/2891398-MIL-has-been-strong-armed-into-signing-a-tenancy-contract
(Quote confirmed by downloading the raw page and searching it directly.)

- **No case where someone had a lawyer or tool review a contract before
  signing and it changed the outcome.** All three new findings describe

- **8 findings were not reached.** I stopped at 4 new findings (plus

## How this changes the V1 picture

The picture shifts in one important way and holds steady in another. It
shifts because the pre-signature moment is no longer purely theoretical: the
MoneySavingExpert post shows a real person spotting a specific risky clause
and asking for help *before* anyone signs — V1's single biggest open
question is now answered "yes, at least sometimes," not "no evidence found."
It also shifts because there is now, for the first time, a first-person lease
story with a named-enough poster and a verifiable quote (Cara Howard), closing
one of V1's two document-type gaps — leases now have real evidence; freelance
agreements still do not. What holds steady is the exit-moment pattern: even
Cara Howard's pre-signature question was overridden by a false reassurance
from the agent, and her real pain only hit at move-out, so "discovered at
exit" is still the dominant shape even in the one clean pre-signature example
found. The freelance-agreement gap remains completely open after real effort
across two rounds of research, which is itself worth treating as a finding:
if a first-person "should I sign this freelance contract" story exists in a
searchable public forum, it has now resisted two independent search efforts
to find it.
