# 0002. Serve freelancers and small business owners, before they sign

Version 1 serves freelancers and small business owners, acts at the
pre-signature moment, and keeps all four document types named in `CLAUDE.md`
(contract, lease, freelance agreement, terms of service). This is the widest of
the options considered on every axis, and it is chosen against the research's
own reading of the evidence, so the reasoning matters more than usual.

**Status is `proposed`, not `accepted`, because the reasoning below is
reconstructed rather than stated.** Correct it before this is relied on.

## Considered options

- **Job candidates reviewing an employment offer.** Rejected. The only segment
  where the pre-signature moment is structurally guaranteed — you cannot start
  the job without signing, and the document arrives by email with a deadline.
  Lawyers charge about $430 for this, giving a clear price to undercut.
- **Renters facing a lease.** Rejected. Sharpest pain story in the research (a
  $1,000 retainer merely to read a lease) but no evidence any renter has ever
  paid for review.
- **Creators reviewing brand deals.** Rejected. Real money ($800–$1,400 per
  review), pool too small to build a first version around.
- **Acting at the exit rather than before signing.** Rejected. Every
  first-person account in the research describes discovering the damage while
  cancelling, moving out, or leaving — not while signing. But at the exit there
  is nothing left to negotiate, and the counter-offer is where Redline creates
  value rather than just explaining a loss.
- **One document type instead of four.** Rejected. Keeps `CLAUDE.md` intact.

## Consequences

- **A free competitor already sits in this segment.** Rocket Lawyer launched a
  free AI contract review for small businesses in August 2025. The brief has to
  answer why someone pays for this instead, and "it is better" is not an answer
  a first version can prove.
- **The best-measured pain in the research is one this product cannot fix.**
  Freelance non-payment affects 71% of freelancers, averaging $5,968 lost a
  year — but only 28% always use a written contract, so for most of that
  segment there is no document to read, and a better-read contract does not
  make a client pay.
- **Four document types spread the clause work four ways.** Employment offers
  and leases share almost no vocabulary. Pact shipped this same breadth to the
  same audience and has two App Store ratings.
- **The timing is a bet, not a finding.** Four researchers found nobody at the
  pre-signature moment. If that moment does not exist, no amount of detection
  accuracy saves the product. Any test plan should attack this first.
